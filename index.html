History of Professional Surfing

Riding waves has likely been going on since humans started to swim in the ocean. Bodysurfing is what started it all while sailors learned how to ride waves on early boats. Riding a surfboard is a more recent development by the Polynesians. The most direct influence on modern surfing is traced back to Hawaii before it was discovered by the rest of the world. Hawaii played a crucial role in the development of professinoal surfing as many contests are still held there today.

Professional surfing began in Hunting Beach, California where the first West Coast Surfing Championship was held. In 1961 the United States Surfing Association was founded. By 1980 brands like town and country surf and body glove became trendy which allowed for pro surfers to make more money and get more exposure. Fast forward to today, only 60 men and 30 women compete at the highest level on the World Surf League and it is now apart of the Olympic Games.
